Mapping the Time: Journeys in Data

A data-literacy project connected to students’ everyday experiences.

Teacher Guide

Project Scenario

Every day, students make a journey that is full of hidden data. In this project, students are encouraged to reflect upon their daily commute to school (or any other place), recording how long it takes, what route they follow, and which mode of transport they use. Over a period of time (i.e. a week), they collect their own “Time-Data” and then manually calculate key statistics (mean, median, and range) to understand the numbers before any digital tool gets involved. This hands-on step is essential: it builds genuine number sense and ensures students can later evaluate whether a GenAI-generated result is trustworthy.

The project also invites students to act as conceptual artists. Using their verified data, they collaborate with a GenAI tool as a creative consultant to transform their geographic commute data into a personalised Time-Map, a visual artwork in which distances are represented by journey time rather than physical space. Students can contribute individual maps to a collective class piece titled Hidden Time-Geography of the City, connecting personal data to shared urban experience. The project sits at the crossroads of mathematics, geography, critical thinking, and visual art, making it genuinely relevant to students’ everyday lives while developing transferable skills in data literacy and AI use.

Important note: the journey itself is not limited to routes to school; it can be any journey that is real, relevant, and meaningful to students. This may include historical journeys, journeys across the mainland or through the air, journeys inspired by literature, or any other type of journey connected to your curriculum (for more, see the Adaptation section).
