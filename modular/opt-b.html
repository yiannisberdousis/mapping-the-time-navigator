OPT B: Build the Time-Map

GenAI role: Optional creative consultant after the visual rule is chosen; not the mapmaker

Modular PathwayOB
C1C2C3OAOB
Teacher Guide

Overview

Overview

Students create a Time-Map: a visual representation in which places are positioned or connected according to travel time and lived mobility rather than conventional geographic distance. Design choices must be traceable to verified data and should make a pattern, contrast or inequality visible rather than merely decorating the map.

Teacher Guide

Expected Student Output

By the end of this part, students should have produced or demonstrated:

  • A Time-Map with a visible legend and a stated time scale or rule.
  • Three annotations linking visual choices to verified data.
  • A short design rationale explaining what the map reveals that a conventional geographic map may hide.
Teacher Guide

Key Teaching Moves / Implementation steps

1. Define the design constraint: time determines spatial relationship.

2. Students choose a scale/rule before drawing.

3. Plot a small number of destinations or journey types using verified values.

4. Add a legend and evidence annotations.

5. Peer test: “Can I reconstruct the rule from the map?”

6. Revise only where the visual encoding is misleading or unclear.

7. Gallery walk with evidence-based feedback.

Teacher Guide

🎨 Creativity Opportunity

Students decide how time changes spatial relationships. They may compress, stretch, cluster, connect or layer places, provided the rule is consistent, visible and evidence-based.

Teacher Guide

🔍 Critical Thinking Prompt

Does the map make the data easier to understand, or does the design accidentally distort the evidence? Which visual choices could lead an audience to the wrong conclusion?

Teacher Guide

Suggested GenAI Prompt for Students

Our Time-Map uses this rule: [state rule]. Our verified finding is: [state finding]. Suggest two ways we could make that relationship easier to see without changing the data or the rule. Explain the trade-off of each suggestion. Do not redesign the map for us.

Teacher Guide

📌 Teacher Note(s)

A Time-Map is not expected to preserve geographic scale. It must preserve its declared temporal rule. Require a legend, units and enough source evidence for another group to understand how positions/links were derived.
